\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Autonomic storage configuration and migration.}
Hippodrome is an early closed-loop storage administrator: it observes workload requirements, searches for a better storage configuration, migrates data, and repeats until requirements are met \citep{anderson2002hippodrome}. Self-managing migration work similarly detects hotspots and minimizes the amount of data copied during reconfiguration \citep{sundaram2006migration}. These systems established the value of observation--action feedback in storage management. Our controller differs in objective and control boundary: it operates continuously inside a file system and treats reliability maintenance as a safety-constrained choice among observation, scrub, rebalance, and proactive drain under uncertain health evidence.

\paragraph{Failure prediction and proactive migration.}
Large field studies show both the promise and limitations of drive-health prediction. Google's study found several SMART correlations but concluded that SMART-only models are unlikely to predict every individual failure \citep{pinheiro2007failure}. RAIDShield combines large-scale disk characterization with active monitoring and proactive replacement of drives predicted to fail \citep{ma2015raidshield}. Later work uses larger datasets and machine learning to improve failure prediction substantially \citep{lu2020smarter}. Proactive data migration has also been studied directly as a way to improve storage availability \citep{wu2015migration}.

A particularly relevant system organizes proactive tolerance as a complete collection--prediction--migration--feedback process and includes secondary validation of predictions \citep{yang2020proactive}. We therefore do not claim that closed-loop proactive storage tolerance is new. Our focus is the file-system action layer after an imperfect signal is available: persistent confirmation and cooldown, a common safety envelope across multiple maintenance actions, foreground-sensitive bounded execution, metadata-conflict handling, and auditable rejected alternatives.

\paragraph{Erasure-coded proactive repair.}
FastPR studies how to perform proactive repair efficiently once a soon-to-fail node is known, coupling migration and reconstruction and parallelizing the repair schedule \citep{li2022fastpr}. Our controller is complementary: it does not propose a faster reconstruction schedule. It decides whether the current evidence justifies a drain, whether the file system can safely admit it, and how that urgent action interacts with scrub/rebalance and foreground pressure. A future implementation could invoke a FastPR-like executor as one action behind the same controller boundary.

\paragraph{Scrubbing and coordinated reliability mechanisms.}
Practical Scrubbing demonstrates that scrub timing and request granularity have major application-performance consequences and designs workload-aware scheduling for the scrubber itself \citep{amvrosiadis2012scrubbing}. FAST'19 work on SSD reliability coordinates re-read, redundancy, and scrubbing because independently enabled reliability techniques can harm performance and lifetime \citep{kim2019ssd}. HeART adapts redundancy choices to reliability heterogeneity observed across disk groups \citep{kadekodi2019heart}. These works share the idea that reliability mechanisms should not be tuned in isolation. Our controller works at a different layer: it coordinates file-system maintenance transitions over devices and file placement while treating existing encoding and scrub/reconstruction mechanisms as primitives.

\paragraph{Maintenance I/O coordination.}
Declarative IO and DINGO show that maintenance tasks such as scrubbing and garbage collection can account for a large fraction of post-cache HDD I/O demand at hyperscalers and that exposing task flexibility enables substantial coordination benefits \citep{athlur2026declarative}. This reinforces the premise that background reliability work is not free. DINGO exposes flexible deadlines and data sets to a distributed storage scheduler; \autopilot{} instead uses a small local feedback controller to decide maintenance action and work-window size. The approaches are compatible: a declarative I/O substrate could replace the prototype's coarse work-unit budget without changing its evidence or safety logic.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
The closest prior systems cover important portions of the same loop: autonomic configuration, prediction, proactive migration, feedback, reliability-mechanism coordination, or maintenance scheduling. The distinct point explored here is their composition at the file-system maintenance boundary. We ask what a controller must retain and check so that a noisy health stream can drive multiple real mutations without giving the predictor authority to bypass redundancy, concurrency, or foreground-pressure constraints.
