\begin{abstract}
Storage systems can observe device health, foreground latency, data placement, and repair outcomes, yet maintenance is commonly exposed as separate reactive or periodic operations. Acting automatically is difficult because health signals are uncertain, maintenance consumes the same I/O resources as applications, and an incorrect action can reduce redundancy at exactly the wrong time. This paper develops \autopilot, a safety-first closed-loop maintenance controller implemented in \argosfs. \autopilot{} combines persistent risk memory, confirmation and cooldown gates, redundancy-aware action checks, conflict-aware mutation stopping, incremental scrub and rebalance, proactive drain, and foreground-aware background-I/O budgets. The evaluation is organized around safety under noisy observations, reduction of risk exposure, foreground interference, and adaptation under changing workloads, with reactive, periodic, fixed-threshold, and oracle policies as comparison points. The current repository establishes the manuscript and reproducible evaluation framework; final quantitative results are reported only after the corresponding experiments are completed.
\end{abstract}
